What a body shop website needs

In bodywork, customers decide by looking: they want to see finished repairs and know whether they can send you a photo before coming in. For most shops, one well-made page is enough.

Your work, with photos

Dents, scratches, painted panels, polished headlights. Real photos from your shop (with the customer's permission and no number plates) say more than any text.

Quotes from a photo

A WhatsApp button so people can send you a photo of the damage from the car park. You decide whether to reply with a rough price or ask them to come in.

Opening hours and directions

Address with a map, opening hours and whether you work by appointment. If you're on an industrial estate, how to find the unit.

What people ask before bringing you their car

It's the same questions again and again. If the website answers them, you get fewer calls asking and more asking for a quote.

“Do you work with my insurer?”

Whether you repair insurance claims, how the assessment works at your shop and what the customer needs to do. Only what's true in your case.

“How much would this cost?”

Ask them to send photos on WhatsApp for a rough price, and explain that the final quote is given in writing once you've seen the car.

“How many days will you need it?”

How long a small and a big job usually take, without committing to dates that depend on parts or assessors.

“Will the colour match?”

How you work on colour and finish, with before-and-after photos. It's what people look at most.

“Do you have a courtesy car?”

Whether you offer one, or whether the insurance covers it. People ask a lot before leaving the car for several days.

“Do you do mechanics too?”

Whether you only do bodywork and paint or also mechanics, glass, headlight polishing or detailing, and what you don't do.

Which sections a body shop website should have (and why)

All on one page designed for mobile: many people open it right after the bump.

  1. Home with what you do and whereBodywork, paint, insurance repairs, polishing… and the area. Anyone who finds you on the map can see at a glance you're the shop they were looking for.
  2. Work with before-and-after photosIt's what convinces most in this trade. With the customer's permission and no visible number plate.
  3. How it works with insuranceSimple steps: claim, assessment, repair and handover. Explained without small print and without promising what depends on the insurer.
  4. Quotes on WhatsAppA button that opens the chat with a message already written, so they only need to attach the photos.
  5. Opening hours and directionsWith a map and a button to get there. And if you collect cars from home, say so clearly.
  6. About youHow long you've been around and who looks after customers. No fluff.

To prepare it, all we need are phone photos of your work, a list of what you do, your opening hours and your WhatsApp number.

Frequently asked questions

Can I show photos of customers' cars?

Yes, with their permission and without showing the number plate or anything that identifies the customer. We tell you how to take the photos so the work shows well.

Can I say I work with insurers?

If you do, yes. It's better to explain how it works at your shop than to use insurers' logos without each one's permission.

Is there any information specific to workshops?

Besides the legal notice, privacy and cookies, you can show your workshop registration number and that you give a written quote before repairing, which Spanish workshop rules recognise as a customer right. If in doubt, your trade association can confirm it.

Can people request a quote from the website?

Yes, on WhatsApp with photos or using the form. The one-page website doesn't include a booking calendar; if you need one, we look at it separately. See the website with bookings.

I also do mechanics, will it all fit?

Yes, each speciality in its own block. If you want each service to appear on Google with its own page, a multi-page website suits you better, and we'll tell you before starting.
