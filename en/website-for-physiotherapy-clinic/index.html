What patients ask before booking

These are the questions that come in again and again by WhatsApp or phone. If the website answers them, patients arrive at their first visit more relaxed and you spend less time on the phone.

“Do you treat my problem?”

A list of treatments under their everyday names (back, knee, pelvic floor, sports injuries…) with one sentence on what each involves.

“How long is a session and how much is it?”

If you want to publish it, the length and the price per session or per pack. If not, one line saying you will tell them by WhatsApp.

“Do I need a doctor's report?”

A short block about the first visit: what to bring, whether a report is needed and how long the initial assessment takes.

“Do you work with my insurer?”

Which health insurers you work with, or that you only see private patients. It is one of the first things people ask.

“Do you do home visits?”

If you do, in which area and in which cases. If you don't, it is worth saying so too.

“Is there parking or a lift?”

Address with a map, how to get there and whether access is step-free: someone with an injury will appreciate it.

Which sections a physiotherapist's website needs (and why)

On a single page, in this order. Each block answers a specific patient question.

  1. Home section with your speciality and areaWithin seconds the patient should know you are a physiotherapist, what you focus on and where you are.
  2. Treatments grouped by problemPatients search for "back pain" or "sprained ankle", not for the name of a technique. Problem first; then how you treat it.
  3. The teamA real photo, name, qualification and registration number for each physiotherapist. It builds trust and, as a regulated profession, it is information that should be shown.
  4. The first visitHow long it lasts, what to bring and how to pay. It reassures people who have never seen a physio.
  5. Opening hours, map and contactWhatsApp and call buttons always visible on mobile, opening hours and directions.
  6. Legal and health detailsLegal notice, privacy, cookies and, if the clinic has one, its health register number.

Before we build your clinic's website

Three things worth being clear about before we start, thinking about how a new patient finds you.

What people search for

Patients usually search by their problem and their area: “physio for back pain”, “sports physio”, “pelvic floor physiotherapy”, “knee rehab”, together with the name of the town or neighbourhood. That is why each treatment has its usual name and a simple sentence about what it involves, and your address is easy to spot.

What we need from you

  • The treatments or specialties you offer, explained in your own words.
  • Who treats patients: name, qualification and professional registration number of each physiotherapist.
  • The clinic's health-authority registration number, if it has one.
  • Opening hours, whether you also do home visits, and whether you work with health insurers or privately only.
  • Real photos of the treatment room, reception and team.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Promising results (“pain gone in three sessions”): in healthcare it undermines trust and may clash with the rules on health advertising.
  • Stock photos of smiling people on a treatment table: patients want to see the clinic they are going to.
  • Not explaining the first visit: how long it lasts, what to bring and whether any medical report is needed. These are the questions that arrive most on WhatsApp.

Frequently asked questions

What legal information does the website need?

Like any business website, a legal notice with your details, a privacy policy and a cookie policy. If you practise a profession that requires membership of a professional association (colegio), Spanish law on information society services also requires you to state the association, your membership number and your qualification. And if your clinic is authorised as a healthcare centre, in many regions its advertising must show the health registry number. We include all of this using the details you give us; if in doubt, your professional association can confirm it.

Can you add patient reviews?

Only real reviews, with permission from the people who wrote them. The simplest option is to link to the reviews on your Google profile. We never make up reviews or add promises of a cure.

Can I explain techniques such as dry needling or osteopathy?

Yes, explained simply and without promising results. We only include the techniques you actually use and, if one requires specific training, we show it as you tell us. We avoid phrases like "cures" or "gets rid of pain", which are best left out of health advertising.

Does it include a booking system?

The one-page website doesn't include its own booking system: patients book via WhatsApp or phone, or we link the button to the online diary you already use. If you need something more complete, we quote it separately. If you want customers to book on their own, see our website with online bookings.

What if we work at several clinics or also do home visits?

It fits on the same page: each clinic with its address, hours and map, and a separate block for home visits with the area you cover. If you have many clinics and want a page for each, we will suggest a multi-page website and tell you before we start.
