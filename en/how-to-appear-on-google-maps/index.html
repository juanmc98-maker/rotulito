In short

It's free

A Google Business Profile costs nothing. Be wary of anyone who charges you just to "put you on Google".

It has to be verified

Google asks you to prove the business exists and that it's yours. Until you verify it, the profile isn't shown normally.

A website reinforces it

A complete profile linked to a website with the same details gives Google, and customers, more reasons to trust you.

Can any business have a profile?

No. Under Google's guidelines, a Business Profile is for businesses that serve customers in person: at premises with permanent signage (a shop, a garage, a hair salon) or by travelling to the customer (a plumber, a gardener). Online-only businesses can't have one.

If you work at customers' homes and don't receive anyone at your address, you can still have a profile as a service-area business: you set the area you cover and your address stays hidden. A PO box or a virtual office where you aren't based doesn't count as an address.

How to get on Google Maps: step by step

  1. Check whether it already exists. Search for your business on Google Maps. Sometimes someone else, or Google itself from public data, has already created it; if so, claim it from the profile ("Own this business?") instead of creating another.
  2. Go to business.google.com with a Google account you'll keep, ideally a business one rather than an employee's personal account. If a person or agency leaves one day, the profile is still yours.
  3. Use the real business name, exactly as on your sign or invoices. Adding words like "cheap" or the town name breaks the rules and can get the profile suspended.
  4. Choose the main category carefully ("Auto repair shop", "Hair salon"…). It has a big influence on which searches you show up in. You can add secondary categories if you really offer those things.
  5. Say where you serve customers. If you have premises, the exact address, and check the pin sits on your door. If you travel to customers, set your service area (towns or region) and hide the address.
  6. Add phone and website. The same phone number you use everywhere and, if you have one, a link to your website.
  7. Verify the profile. Google decides which methods to offer you: phone or text, email, recording a video of your premises, sign and tools, or a live video call. According to Google, the review can take up to 5 business days. Meanwhile, avoid changing the name, address or category.

What to fill in so it works

  • Opening hoursReal and up to date, including holidays. Few things annoy people more than turning up to find you closed.
  • Phone and websiteThe same phone as on your website, plus a link to it. That way customers and Google see everything matches.
  • ServicesWhat you do, in the words your customers use ("oil change", "hair colour", "bathroom refit").
  • Real photosOf the outside (so people recognise you when they arrive), the inside, your team and your work. A few good ones beat lots of dark ones.
  • Service areaIf you travel to customers, the towns or area you really cover.
  • DescriptionTwo or three clear sentences about what you do and for whom, with no links or keyword lists.

After verification

A profile isn't something you set up and forget. What helps over time:

  • New photos now and then. A finished job, a new product, your shop window in another season.
  • Updates. You can post updates, offers or events from the profile. They don't work miracles, but they show customers the business is active.
  • Check suggested edits. Anyone can suggest changes to your profile (hours, phone, even that you've closed) and Google may apply them. Log in from time to time and check everything is still right.
  • Answer messages and questions if you've turned them on. If you can't reply, it's better to switch messaging off than leave people waiting.
  • Look at the stats. The profile shows how many calls, website visits and direction requests it gets. It's the simplest way to see whether it's working for you.

Reviews: ask for them, never buy them

Reviews matter a lot, to Google and to customers. You can ask customers for them, for example with a link or a QR code on the counter or the invoice. What Google doesn't allow is offering discounts or gifts in exchange for a review, or posting fake reviews: it may remove them or penalise the profile.

Reply to reviews, including bad ones, calmly and politely. Whoever reads your reply is your next customer.

Common mistakes

  • Creating a second profile instead of claiming the existing one. Duplicates get mixed up and can end up suspended.
  • Stuffing keywords into the name ("Smith Garage cheap mechanic York").
  • Showing your home address when you don't receive customers there.
  • Leaving summer hours all year or not marking holidays.
  • Leaving the profile in someone else's account (an employee, a relative, an agency) and losing access when they leave.
  • Changing lots of details at once right after verifying: Google may review the profile again.
  • Buying reviews or asking relatives who were never customers to leave them.

Appearing for free vs. advertising on Google Maps

Showing up on the map with your profile is free. "Advertising on Google Maps" means something else: Google Ads, which appear labelled "Sponsored" on the map and in search results and are paid separately (usually per click or action, with a budget you set). To show your address in the ad, your profile is linked to your Google Ads account.

Paying for ads doesn't improve your free ranking: they're separate. For most small businesses, the first step is a complete, up-to-date profile; ads, if you want them, come later.

What decides your position

Google explains that local results depend mainly on three things: relevance (how well your profile matches the search), distance (how close you are to the searcher or the place mentioned) and prominence (how well known your business is: reviews, mentions, your website). Google also says you can't pay to improve your local ranking. That's why nobody can guarantee you the top spot; what you can control is a complete, accurate, well-kept profile.

Profile and website: better together

Your Google profile is the quick shop window; your website is where a hesitant customer calmly reads what you do, sees your work and gets in touch. When the profile links to a website with the same name, address and phone number, everything fits. And on the profile you don't fully control what is shown; on your website, you do.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to appear on Google Maps?

A Google Business Profile is free. What you may pay for, if you want, is Google Ads, or someone to help you set it up and keep it updated.

How do I get my business on Google Maps?

Create your Business Profile at business.google.com (or claim the existing one), fill in your real name, category, address or service area, hours and phone, and verify it with the method Google offers you.

What is the difference between appearing and advertising on Google Maps?

Appearing with your profile is free. Advertising means paying for Google Ads, which are labelled "Sponsored". Paying for ads does not improve your free ranking.

I work from home. Do I have to show my address?

If you don't receive customers at home, no. Register as a service-area business, set the area you cover and the address stays hidden.

How long does it take to appear?

According to Google, the verification review can take up to 5 business days, sometimes longer. After that, which searches you appear in depends on relevance, distance and how well known your business is.

I have a profile but I don't show up. Why?

It may not be verified, the category may not match what people search for, or the searcher may be far away. Also check there's no duplicate profile and that the map pin is in the right place.

Can I put my town name in the business name?

Only if it is part of your real name. Adding keywords to the name is against Google's guidelines.

Do I need a website if I already have a profile?

It isn't required, but it helps: a website explains what doesn't fit in the profile, it's yours, and it backs up the profile's details.
