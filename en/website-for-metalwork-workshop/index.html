What a metalwork workshop website needs

Anyone looking for a door, an enclosure or a railing wants to see jobs like theirs and know whether you cover their town. A clear single page does that; if you make many different products and want each one on Google, a multi-page website suits you better.

Your work, by type

Garage doors, aluminium windows, enclosures, railings, grilles, structures. With real photos of finished jobs: that's what convinces people most.

Where you work

The towns and districts where you install. It's the first question from anyone who finds you online, and it saves calls you don't want.

Straightforward quotes

A WhatsApp button so people can send rough measurements and photos of the opening. You decide whether to give a rough price or go and measure.

What people ask before requesting a quote

If the website answers it, you get clearer enquiries and waste less time on visits that go nowhere.

“Do you come to my town?”

The area you cover, with town and district names, and whether you charge for travel outside it.

“Do you do motorised garage doors?”

One line for each thing you make or install, using the name people search for.

“Aluminium, iron or stainless steel?”

Which materials you work with and what you recommend each for, without jargon.

“Can you price it on WhatsApp?”

Ask for measurements and photos for a rough price, and explain the final quote is in writing after measuring.

“How long does installation take?”

Usual manufacturing and installation times, as a guide and without committing to fixed dates.

“Do you do repairs or only new work?”

Whether you fix locks, motors or shutters, or only do made-to-measure work.

Which sections a metalwork workshop website should have (and why)

All on one page that reads well on a phone, because many enquiries come from the building site itself.

  1. Home with what you do and whereMetalwork, aluminium, locksmithing… and the district. Anyone who finds you can see at a glance if you're what they need.
  2. Work by type, with photosDoors, windows, enclosures, railings and structures, each with two or three photos of real jobs.
  3. Materials and finishesAluminium, iron, steel, coatings and colours. Just enough for customers to arrive knowing what they want.
  4. Service areaThe towns where you install, written out by name: it also helps people find you from there.
  5. Quotes on WhatsApp or by formWith a pre-written message so they only need to add measurements and photos.
  6. About youHow long you've been around, whether you have your own workshop and who looks after customers.

To prepare it, all we need are phone photos of your work, a list of what you make and install, the towns you cover and your WhatsApp number.

Frequently asked questions

Can I show prices?

If you like, “from” prices for the most common jobs. Almost everything in this trade is made to measure, so it's usual to invite people to ask for a quote with measurements and photos.

Do I need to mention CE marking for doors and windows?

If you make doors, windows or enclosures that carry it, you can mention it on the website, but what counts is the documentation you hand over with each product. We add it with the details you give us; if in doubt, your trade association can confirm it. This is guidance, not advice.

Can I show photos of customers' homes?

Yes, with their permission and without showing the address or anything that identifies the property. Close-ups of the work are better than the whole façade.

I work in many towns, will I appear in all of them?

We list the towns and districts you cover on the website. Nobody can guarantee Google rankings; if you want to work your area in more depth, we can look at local SEO.

What if I already have an old website?

We rebuild it keeping your domain, so you don't lose what you already have. If you like, we'll first review it for free.
